# Narsil commentary: LinkedIn and X

Commentary is Nate's voice in public: first person, bylined, opinions allowed. Same temperament as the site, with more "I" and fewer CTAs. Read SKILL.md first. This file adds what's specific to social.

## What commentary is for

- Reacting to search and AI news with what it means for a business owner.
- Sharing what we see in the work, with real numbers or none.
- Giving away one piece of method small enough for a post.

Most posts carry no CTA. When one points somewhere, it points to an article or free tool, placed last. Brand is still named per piece: a post about a Narsil SEO feature follows the software column of the brand table; everything else defaults to Nate speaking as the founder of Narsil Creative.

## Rules

- **The first line is the claim.** It has to stand on its own before the "see more" cut, so it can't be a teaser ("I was wrong about something.") or a question. Put the answer there.
- **News reactions follow a fixed order.** Link the primary source (Google Search Central, the company's own post, the documentation), name the date, state what changed in one sentence, then what it means for a small business, then what to do this week. Often the answer is nothing, and saying so is the post. Label speculation as speculation.
- **Against-interest is the best material we have.** "This changes nothing for most local businesses" is a useful post.
- **Numbers from the work need a source.** Client results need real numbers and a client who has agreed to be named; otherwise describe the pattern without the client.
- **Disagree with claims, never sneer at people.** Name the claim; don't dunk on whoever made it. Never mock businesses that haven't caught up.
- **Replies on other people's posts** add a specific fact, a counterexample, or a question that moves the thread. Never "Great post!" or "This."

## Banned on social

Broetry (stacks of one-sentence paragraphs; mix lengths the way the site does). Engagement bait ("Agree?", "Thoughts?", "Comment AUDIT and I'll DM you," "Repost if..."). Announcement theater ("I'm thrilled to announce," "Humbled to share"). Padded numbered-lessons posts ("7 lessons from...") where the count came before the lessons. Emoji or arrow bullets. Hashtag walls (zero to three, only real topics). Unicode "bold" text, which screen readers can't read. Thread markers like "🧵" or "A thread 👇".

## Format by platform

**LinkedIn.** Plain text. Most posts run 80-250 words, with a blank line between paragraphs. A short list only when the items are real steps. Link placement is Nate's call per post.

**X.** A single post under 280 characters by default. A thread only when each post stands on its own and the idea needs the room; number posts only if order matters.

## Before posting

- First line answers or claims.
- Primary source linked for any news or stat.
- One contrast at most, zero dashes as connectors, no staged candor, no engagement-bait closer.
- Nate would say it out loud to a smart friend in the industry.
