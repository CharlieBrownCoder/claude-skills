# Narsil emails

Email runs on the full Narsil voice with two adjustments: it's shorter, and it's written to one person. Read SKILL.md first. This file adds what's specific to email.

## Rules for every email

- **The subject line says what's inside.** Specific, sentence case, short enough to read in a phone preview. No emoji, no clickbait, no "Quick question," no fake "Re:" or "Fwd:".
- **The first sentence is the point:** the result, the answer, the ask, or the bad news.
- **One ask per email**, stated plainly, with a date if there is one.
- **Plain text by default** for anything sent from a person. Short paragraphs, contractions, no images or heavy HTML in relationship email.
- **Sign off with a name.** "Nate" for anything from Nate. A plain "Thanks," is fine; skip stacked sign-offs.
- **End on the ask**, not on an offer to answer questions.
- Every rule in SKILL.md still applies: no dashes as connectors, the contrast cap, no staged candor, the merged vocabulary.

Banned in email: "I hope this finds you well," "I wanted to reach out," "just checking in," "circling back," "touching base," "per my last email," "please don't hesitate," "at your earliest convenience," "let me know if you have any questions," "bumping this to the top of your inbox," "did you see my last email."

## Customer and client emails

### Agency client emails (Narsil Creative)

The weekly update email is a published promise, so it's the brand's proof in the inbox. Keep the order fixed so clients learn where to look.

```
Subject: Week [n]: [what shipped], [what moved]

1. The one thing that matters this week, good or bad.
2. What shipped: pages, placements, fixes, with links.
3. What moved: numbers, their source (Search Console, GA4,
   AI citation tests, GBP insights), and the baseline.
4. What didn't move, and whether that's expected at this
   point on the months 3-6 curve.
5. What's next week.
6. What we need from you, with a date. (Omit if nothing.)
```

Bad news goes first and arrives with its plan in the same email. Never bury a missed deliverable under wins. If a number is too early or too noisy to read, say so instead of spinning it. Aim for 150-300 words; the live dashboard carries the detail.

Other client moments:

- **Onboarding.** What happens in the first two weeks, what we need from them and by when, and when results typically show (months 3-6). Set the timeline in the first email, not the fifth.
- **End of the initial commitment.** Results against the starting baseline, in numbers. If continuing isn't worth it for them, say so.
- **Our mistakes.** Own it in the first line. Then what happened, what we've already done, and what changes so it doesn't repeat.

### Narsil SEO product emails (software)

Full microcopy register: what happened, what it means, the one next step.

- **Welcome.** The first thing to do (add the site; the first audit runs), when results appear, and where the fix guides live.
- **Audit ready and weekly digests.** Lead with the score change and the top issue, linked to its fix. Don't pad with issues that didn't change.
- **Billing.** Exact amounts and dates. Announce price changes with the reason; the Pro cut from $139 to $99, with every subscriber moved down automatically, is the model.
- **Cancellation.** Confirm it and state exactly what happens to their account and history (check the facts file). No guilt, no "we're sad to see you go," no save-offer tricks. One optional line inviting a reply about what would have kept them.
- **Support replies.** Answer first. If it's our bug, say so in the first line. Give the exact next step and when they'll hear back.

Never imply a free trial or free tier; there isn't one. Never promise rankings. Keep transactional emails under about 120 words; digests can run longer when the data earns it. Sender name and reply-to are Nate's call per sequence; ask if they aren't set.

## Outreach emails

Most agency outreach is the worst writing in the industry. Narsil outreach earns the reply by giving something specific and checkable first.

### Cold outreach to prospects

- **Open with a finding you verified yourself** about their business, such as what an AI assistant returned when asked for their category in their city. Name the assistant, the exact prompt, and the date. Never invent or inflate a finding. If you didn't run the check, you don't have a first line.
- **Give the finding away:** what it means and the first fix, free.
- **Include the against-interest line:** when they don't need us ("If someone's already on this, ignore me.").
- **One CTA:** the free AI Visibility Audit, or a reply. No calendar link as the first ask.
- **No fake personalization or flattery** ("loved your website," "I noticed you're growing fast").
- **One follow-up at most**, and only if it adds something new: a second finding, a relevant article.
- **Follow commercial email basics** (CAN-SPAM in the US): a working opt-out, a physical mailing address, and a subject line that matches the body.

Aim for 60-120 words.

### Link, partner, and digital PR outreach

- Lead with what's useful to them: the gap on their page your resource fills, data they can cite, or an outdated claim on their site you can help correct.
- Reference only pages you've read. Name the page and the section.
- Pitch real assets: Narsil articles, free tools and kits, and original data. Confirm each exists in the facts file before naming it.
- **Clean hands:** never offer payment, a link swap, or anything of value for a link. Exchanged links violate Google's spam policies on link schemes and break the brand's clean-hands rule. If a site asks for payment, walk away.

### Podcast and speaking pitches

- Founder-to-host register: professional and human.
- Reference a specific episode only if you've verified it (title, guest, date).
- Pitch two topics that fit the show's audience, each stated as a claim Nate can defend with specifics.
- Credentials in one line, from the facts file's bio, with its framing.
- One ask. Offer a lighter alternative (a quote, a guest post) only if it fits the show.
