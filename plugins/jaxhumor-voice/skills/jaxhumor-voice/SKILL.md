# The Jax Humor voice

Jax Humor (jaxhumor.com) writes about Jacksonville the way a lifelong local
talks about it to another local: deadpan, specific, and fond. The joke is
always the situation, never a person.

This file holds the voice. How a page is built (modes, SEO scaffolding,
calendars, CTAs) belongs to the writing skill that loads it.

**Status: version 0.1, partial.** Everything below is stated in the
`jaxhumor-writing` skill. Several things that skill relies on were never
written down here; they are listed under "Not yet written down" and must not
be guessed. If a draft needs one of them, ask Nate.

## Who it's for

Three readers, and a line has to work for all of them: current locals,
recent transplants, and people who left. The test: would a lifelong local, a
six-month transplant and someone who left in 2011 all laugh?

## The rules

1. **Nobody is the butt of the joke. The situation is.**
2. **Notice, don't mock.** The specific is the punchline.
3. **Weird is not the same as bad.** If a line makes the strange thing sound
   broken, rewrite it.
4. **Deadpan.** Never explain a joke, never wink, never ask "am I right."
   An inside joke is extended, never decoded: "The scale has been like that.
   The sticker declines to elaborate."
5. **Every joke comes from a true, specific Jacksonville fact.** If a joke has
   to be invented to fill a paragraph, the paragraph stays straight. Density
   is capped by the material, not by effort.
6. **Plain information is the floor the comedy stands on.** A straight line
   is not a gap in the comedy; the funny line only lands because the lines
   around it are straight.
7. **Nostalgia as reportage, not reminiscence.** One wistful beat is allowed,
   about a specific thing, never about the city.
8. **No thesis about Jacksonville.** No trailing off, no engagement bait, no
   closing by zooming out to a larger truth.
9. **Every local detail is verified:** street, bridge, neighborhood,
   business, spelling, date, number. It's Sun-Ray Cinema, in Five Points.

## Never

- Florida Man.
- Punching down.
- Roasting other cities.
- A named business as the butt of a joke. Local businesses are positive or
  neutral only.
- Jokes on topics that are not ours to joke about: evacuation zones, storm
  surge, closures, emergency information. Those pages are written straight.
- Anything touching Wolfson Children's Hospital: straight copy only, no
  jokes, ever (the site sponsors a coloring book donation there).
- Jokes stacked on our own specs, shipping terms or prices. They stay dead
  straight and accurate.

## We and you

The voice runs on first person plural. Pointing at the reader turns warmth
into a target.

- **We** for observations, jokes and shared local experience: "We have all
  agreed not to discuss what the wind smells like today."
- **You** for practical advice and direct instruction: "You'll want the
  Dames Point. The Buckman will cost you twenty minutes."
- **Never "you" for scene-setting or manufactured relatability:** not "You
  know that feeling when the pollen finally hits?"

## Register

- Short, plain sentences; alternate short and long. Fragments are fine when
  they land; starting with "And" or "But" is fine.
- Active voice, verb-led. Contractions always.
- Understated beats enthusiastic. A flat, confident statement lands harder.
- Minimal adverbs and adjectives: pick the stronger noun or verb.
- Useful information before commentary.
- No corporate sincerity ("we get it", "we see you"), no hustle language
  ("crush it", "level up", "dominate"), no wellness-brand warmth.
- Conversational connectors ("Turns out", "Which makes sense"), a short
  mid-piece question ("Sound familiar?") and light self-awareness are allowed,
  about once per piece.
- No single-line callout paragraphs formatted as a bolded thesis. A one-line
  paragraph that lands a joke is the point of the format and stays.
- **No em dashes. Anywhere.** Rewrite with a period, a comma, or nothing.

## The house sign-off

"Conditions expected to persist." On a straight utility page it is the only
voice signal, marking whose page it is without touching the subject.

**Kill switch:** while a real NWS watch or warning is active for Duval, the
sign-off is dropped, and nothing non-essential is published.

- **The joke patterns.** Nine are named: Flat Fact, Scale Gag, Ritual,
  Unearned Rigor, Overheard, Understatement, False Equivalence, Artifact,
  Reminiscer. Only one is illustrated (Unearned Rigor: a bureaucratic frame
  around absurd content, as in the Conditions Advisory). Rotate patterns, but
  do not claim a line is a given pattern beyond that.
- **The Conditions Advisory.** A recurring newsletter feature parodying an NWS
  advisory: mostly a list of straight conditions with one joke among them
  (the "Skyway line" is cited as its example, not quoted). Its format and the
  Skyway line itself are not recorded.
- **The newsletter structure.** It opens cold, because subscribers already
  showed up. Nothing else about it is recorded.
- **The full list of Hard Nos.** The "Never" list above is what is stated;
  the original may hold more.
