# Manifest Voice & Copy Skill

Use this skill to produce any customer-facing content for Manifest — the goal-sharing app built around trusted circles, accountability through real relationships, and what we call the Personal Growth Economy. This is the authoritative source for brand voice, writing rules, and content standards.

---

## Who Manifest Talks To

**Primary audience:** Adults in their 20s to 50s who are actively building — careers, finances, health routines, relationships, side projects, businesses. They want to grow but they're doing most of it alone.

**Their mindset:**
- Motivated but often isolated in their goals
- Skeptical of social media ("I don't want to perform my progress")
- Burned by solo accountability systems that don't stick
- Open to help from their real network — but unsure how to ask
- Value trust and privacy over reach and likes

**What they want to feel:** Supported, seen by people who matter, and like their goals are finally moving somewhere real.

---

## The Brand in One Line

> *Most goal apps are built for solo discipline. Manifest is built for the people around you.*

This contrast — solo vs. networked, broadcast vs. trusted, performance vs. progress — is the emotional engine behind all content.

## Voice Principles (The Three Non-Negotiables)

### Human
Warm, grounded, and real. Write like a smart friend who's done some thinking, not a wellness brand or a startup pitching investors. No corporate warmth. No manufactured empathy. Real.

### Honest
Say what's true, even when it's uncomfortable. Manifest doesn't pretend goal-setting is easy or that support always shows up. Acknowledge the messy middle. Trust the reader.

### Momentum-Focused
Every piece of content should leave the reader one step closer to action. Not inspired to act someday — ready to act now. Specific, tangible, and forward-moving.

## Writing Style Rules

### Active & Direct
- ✅ "Write the goal. Share it. Let people help."
- ❌ "Goals may be more achievable when shared with others."

Write as if already in motion. Start sentences with verbs where possible. Never passive voice when active is available.

### Burstiness — Alternate Short and Long Sentences
Short sentences land hard. Longer sentences carry nuance, context, and texture that lets an idea breathe before the next short one hits.

- ✅ "Most people don't fail because they can't work hard. They fail because their goal lives in their head, their progress is invisible, and they're trying to do it solo."
- ❌ "Many individuals who do not achieve their goals often experience challenges related to isolation and a lack of a structured support system."

### Punchy & Concise
- ✅ "Vague goals get vague help."
- ❌ "When a goal lacks specificity, it becomes difficult for others in your network to provide targeted and meaningful assistance."

Cut every word that isn't working. Clarity is kindness.

### Earned Enthusiasm
- ✅ "That's the shift. From broadcasting to belonging."
- ❌ "This incredible platform is changing the way people think about goals!"

Don't manufacture energy. When something matters, say so plainly and let it land.

### Minimal Adverbs
Adverbs are usually a sign that the verb is weak. Strengthen the verb instead.
- ✅ "Goals collapse."
- ❌ "Goals often quietly fail."

## Grammar & Mechanics

| Rule | Guidance |
|---|---|
| **Brand name** | Always "Manifest" — capital M. Never "manifest" (lowercase) in brand contexts. The app is "Manifest" or "the Manifest app." |
| **Contractions** | Always. "You're" not "You are." "Let's" not "Let us." "It's" not "It is." |
| **Exclamation points** | Encouraged for genuine moments — not sprinkled throughout. Max one per paragraph. |
| **Em dashes** | Never. Rewrite the sentence or use a period, comma, or parentheses instead. |
| **Capitalization** | Sentence case for most text. Product concepts (Success Network, Personal Growth Economy, Personal Growth Flywheel, Recognition Loop) are capitalized when used as proper terms. |
| **Jargon** | Never. No "synergy," "leverage," "utilize," "growth hacking," or wellness-speak. Write for a smart human, not a pitch deck. |
| **Filler phrases** | Never use: "I hope this finds you well," "please don't hesitate to reach out," "at your earliest convenience," "as per my last email," "did you know?" |
| **Stats** | Always name the source. If a stat can't be sourced, flag it or cut it — don't publish it. |
| **Product tie-ins** | Earned and organic. Place naturally at the end of a piece, never forced throughout. One clean mention is worth ten awkward ones. |

## Tone by Context

Voice stays human and honest — tone adapts to the moment.

| Context | Tone | Example |
|---|---|---
| Blog / editorial | Relaxed, conversational, slightly personal | "Here's what nobody tells you about accountability..." |
| Goal progress / celebration | Warm, grounded, genuine | "You followed through. That matters more than you know." |
| Error / friction | Empathetic, solution-focused, not dramatic | "Something went wrong. Let's sort it out." |
| Re-engagement / nudge | Warm, low pressure, specific | "You haven't updated this goal in a while. How's it going?" |
| Product / feature explanation | Clear and plain — benefits over features | "Manifest keeps your goal and the people around it in one place — no more repeating yourself." |
| Social posts | Short, punchy, human — no hustle-culture energy | "Your network can't help a goal they can't see." |
| Trust / privacy context | Honest and direct — no overselling | "Your goals are only visible to the people you invite. That's it." |

## Blog & Article-Specific Rules

### Intros
- Hook with a stat, quote, or punchy observation. Never "did you know?"
- First sentence does the work. No warm-up, no "in today's world."
- One idea per intro paragraph.

### Article Structure
1. **Hook** — Stat, quote, or sharp observation that earns the read.
2. **The Problem** — Name the tension or gap clearly. Don't lecture.
3. **The Insight** — One core idea, developed well.
4. **The Practical Part** — Specific, actionable, concrete.
5. **The Close** — Forward-looking. Not a summary. A landing.
6. **Manifest tie-in** — Optional, earned, placed at the end. Natural, not salesy.

### SEO Requirements
Every article must include:
- Meta title
- Meta description
- Slug
- Excerpt

### Length
Blog articles target 700–1,100 words. Every paragraph earns its place.

### Disclaimers
- Financial content (debt, saving, investing): include a brief note that this is not personal financial advice.
- Mental health content: include professional-help framing and a mention of support resources.

## Email-Specific Rules

### Subject Lines
- Under 50 characters where possible
- Lead with outcome or action, not process
- No clickbait, no false urgency
- ✅ "Your goal circle is waiting"
- ✅ "How's that goal coming?"
- ❌ "Important information regarding your Manifest account"

### Email Structure (in order)
1. **Hook** — First line does the work. No warm-up.
2. **The What** — What happened or what's needed. One idea per email.
3. **The Why** — Brief human reason it matters to them.
4. **CTA** — Single, verb-led, clear. Never two competing CTAs.
5. **Sign-off** — Warm and on-brand.

### CTA Copy
- ✅ "Share your goal" / "Add your first milestone" / "Invite your circle" / "See how it works"
- ❌ "Click here" / "Submit" / "Proceed" / "Learn more" / "Continue"

### Email Length by Type

| Type | Length |
|---|---|
| Re-engagement / nudge | 3–5 sentences max. Single CTA. |
| Confirmation | Medium — confirm, add helpful context, single CTA |
| Onboarding / welcome | Medium-long — step-by-step clarity over brevity |
| Educational / nurture | Can be longer — every paragraph must earn its place |
| Transactional | Structured and scannable — bullet lists where helpful |

## Manifest Never Does This

- Passive voice ("has been completed," "will be processed")
- "I hope this email finds you well" or any variant
- Em dashes — rewrite the sentence instead
- "Did you know?" as an article opener
- Manufactured enthusiasm or wellness-brand warmth
- Walls of unbroken text
- Forced Manifest product mentions mid-article
- Unsourced statistics — flag them or cut them
- Hustle-culture language ("crush your goals," "dominate your habits," "level up")
- Shame or guilt for not completing a step
- More than one CTA competing in the same email
- Corporate filler: "please don't hesitate," "as per my last email," "at your earliest convenience"
- Overpromising on privacy or outcomes
- Adverb stacking ("very quickly," "extremely easily," "highly effectively")

## Pre-Write Checklist

Before drafting any Manifest content, confirm:

- [ ] What is this content for? (Blog, email, social, in-app?)
- [ ] Who is the reader and what stage are they at? (Prospective user, new user, active user?)
- [ ] What ONE thing should they feel or do after reading?
- [ ] Does the intro hook without "did you know?" or a warm-up sentence?
- [ ] Are sentences alternating short and long for burstiness?
- [ ] Are adverbs minimal — verbs doing the work instead?
- [ ] Is the Manifest tie-in earned and placed at the end — not forced throughout?
- [ ] Are all stats sourced by name?
- [ ] Is the brand name written as "Manifest" throughout?
- [ ] No em dashes anywhere?
- [ ] Would the reader know exactly what to think or do after finishing?

## Voice Rewrites — Reference Examples

### Blog Intro
❌ "In today's fast-paced world, many people struggle to achieve their goals without the right support system in place."
✅ "Eighty percent of New Year's resolutions fail by February. Not because people are lazy. Because they're trying to do it alone."

### Vague Goal → Clear Goal
❌ "Work on my career"
✅ "Identify two roles that match my strengths and talk to people already doing them — this quarter."

### Product Mention (forced)
❌ "Using Manifest, you can share your goals with your trusted circle, track milestones, and receive nudges from your network, making Manifest the perfect tool for anyone who wants to achieve their goals with real people."
✅ "That's exactly what Manifest is built for — keeping your goal, your circle, and your momentum in one place."

### Re-engagement Nudge
❌ "We noticed you haven't updated your goal recently. Please return to the app to continue your progress."
✅ "It's been a while since your last update. How's the goal going? Your circle is still with you."

### Privacy Explanation
❌ "Manifest takes your privacy very seriously and ensures that your personal goal data remains completely secure."
✅ "Your goals are only visible to the people you invite. No algorithm. No public feed. Just your circle."

### Social Post
❌ "Share your goals on Manifest today and let your network help you achieve them! Sign up now!"
✅ "Your network can't help a goal they can't see. Make it visible to the right people."

### Subject Lines
❌ "Important update regarding your Manifest account activity"
✅ "How's that goal going?"

## Key Manifest Concepts (Use These Accurately)

| Term | What It Means |
|---|---|
| **Success Network** | The small, trusted circle of people around a goal — not a broad social network |
| **Personal Growth Economy** | The idea that goals, support, and recognition can circulate as real value between people |
| **Personal Growth Flywheel** | The cycle of setting a goal, sharing progress, receiving support, completing, recognizing helpers, and starting again |
| **Recognition Loop** | The closing phase where a completed goal triggers recognition for the people who helped |
| **Goal Circle** | The specific group of people invited to support a particular goal |
| **Nudge** | A lightweight, supportive prompt — from the network or the system — when activity slows |
| **Milestone** | A signal of progress, not a checkpoint verdict — designed to invite support, not judge completion |
