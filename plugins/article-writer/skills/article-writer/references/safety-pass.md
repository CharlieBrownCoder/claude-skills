# The safety pass

Run before drafting, in this order. Write down each call; the report lists
them.

## 1. Overlap (do we already have this?)

Search the profile's published and drafts folders for the keyword, its
plural and singular, and two or three close variants (in titles, slugs,
keyword fields and H2s).

- A page already serves the same intent: stop. Propose a refresh of that
  page, or a different angle with a different keyword. Two pages for one
  intent compete with each other.
- A page covers a neighbouring intent: go on, and link to it.
- Also search for the piece's best lines. A line already used in another
  article (an aphorism, a framing) is not reused, unless the profile lists it
  as an approved positioning line.

## 2. The source (playbook and refresh modes)

- Drop everything under a heading that marks material internal, such as
  `## Source transcript (INTERNAL — never ships)`. Read the source above that
  heading only. The draft never quotes a transcript.
- If the profile or the source library keeps a grey-area or banned-tactics
  list, check the source's tactics against it. A grey-area tactic does not
  appear in the piece, not even as a warning that teaches it.
- Attribution: if the piece teaches a method someone else taught publicly,
  follow the profile's attribution rule. With no rule, credit the source by
  name in the body. Record the method's provenance in the source log either
  way.
- The source's own figures: a case-study number with no named, consenting
  client and date does not ship, and no figure is ever presented as a
  result the reader can expect.

## 3. Freshness

List every claim about a platform's current behaviour (a feature, a report,
a policy, a price, a limit, a deprecation). Check each against the
platform's own documentation today, and log the URL and what it says. A
claim the documentation no longer supports is cut or corrected, and the
change is reported. A source document's date is not evidence that it is
still true.

## 4. Proof and promises

- Statistics: a named primary source you opened, or cut. "Studies show",
  "industry estimates" and "reportedly" are not sources.
- No promised rankings, citations, rich results, traffic or timeframes.
  Where a timeline helps, state the real range and its conditions.

## 5. Brand rules

Apply every rule in the profile's "Rules this brand adds" section, for
example a ban on naming competing tools.

## What stops the piece

Stop and tell the user, rather than drafting around it, when:

- an existing page already serves the intent (step 1);
- the source rests on a grey-area tactic, or its method cannot be
  attributed and the profile requires attribution (step 2);
- the central claim fails freshness (step 3).
