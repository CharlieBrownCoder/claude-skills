# The method

How the piece is built, for every brand. The voice skill decides how it
sounds; this file decides what it does.

## 1. Answer first

The first sentence answers the title's question or states the thesis. No
warm-up, no restating the question, no "in this article". A TL;DR, if the
profile's template has one, is the answer in two or three sentences. Every
section opens with its point, then supports it.

## 2. The five placements

The keyword appears, naturally and as the reader types it, in:

1. the title, at or near the front;
2. the meta description, early, within the profile's limit;
3. the slug;
4. the H1 (usually the title);
5. the first sentence, near its start.

That is the floor, not a density target. Use variants in H2s where they read
naturally; never repeat the exact phrase to hit a count.

## 3. Match, then add

Before outlining, read the pages that rank for the keyword today (the top
three at least). List the subtopics and questions they share; the piece
covers all of them. Then add what none of them has. Matching earns parity;
the addition is the reason a reader, or a search engine, prefers this page:

- first-hand detail: what happened when the owner did it, what went wrong;
- data the brand collected, with its source;
- a tool, template or worked example the reader can use;
- a clearer format: a table where they wrote paragraphs, steps where they
  wrote theory.

If the brief cannot name an addition, say so before drafting.

## 4. Structure

- H1 from the title; H2s in the profile's case; H3s only under an H2.
- Short paragraphs: two to four sentences. Mix sentence lengths.
- One list style that carries bold leads per piece at most; other lists are
  plain.
- Headings say what the section answers, so the outline alone reads as a
  summary.

## 5. One against-interest moment

Every substantial piece says one thing a salesperson would leave out: who
should not do this, what it really costs, how long it really takes, when the
free option is enough. Demonstrate candour; never label it.

## 6. One giveaway

One thing the reader can copy and keep: a checklist, a template, a prompt, a
regex, a decision table. Make it complete enough to use without hiring
anyone.

## 7. Claims

- A statistic carries its named primary source, or it is cut.
- A platform fact is checked against the platform's own documentation at
  draft time (the safety pass, step 3).
- No promised outcome or timeframe. Where a timeline helps, give the real
  range and what it depends on.

## 8. The service mention

Once, near the end, where it follows from the piece. Never salted through
the body. If the profile's template adds a CTA, the body carries none.

## 9. FAQs

When the profile asks for them: questions phrased the way people search,
answers of two to four sentences that stand alone, plus any brand FAQ the
profile requires. Never promise FAQ rich results.

## 10. Reads human

The voice skill's rules apply in full. The core adds only what
`check_article.py` enforces everywhere: no em dashes and the core banned
words (slop vocabulary). Then read the draft aloud in your head once: cut
anything that only restates, and any sentence that would fit any article on
any site.
