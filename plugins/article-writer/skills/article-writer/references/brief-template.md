# The brief

Fill every field before drafting. A field you decided yourself carries
"(assumed)" so the user can overrule it in one reply. Fields that map to
frontmatter use the profile's key names.

```markdown
## Brief: <working title>

- Mode: playbook | question | refresh
- Source: <file path, the customer question, or the article being refreshed>
- Keyword: <the one target phrase, as a reader types it>
- Search intent: <what the reader wants to walk away with, one sentence>
- The answer: <the first sentence of the article, written now>
- Reader: <from the profile, narrowed to this piece if useful>
- Title: <keyword at or near the front>
- Slug: <short, keyword-bearing>
- Meta description: <answer-first, within the profile's limit; write it to
  the limit now, do not trim it later>
- Category, byline, length: <per the profile>
- What the ranking pages cover: <the subtopics the top results share>
- What we add that they lack: <first-hand detail, data, a tool, a better
  format; if nothing, say so: the piece may not be worth writing>
- Against-interest moment: <who should not do this, the real timeline, the
  cost we would rather not mention>
- Giveaway: <the one thing the reader can copy: a checklist, template,
  prompt, regex, table>
- Internal links: <candidate pages, with their dates when date causality
  applies>
- Service mention: <the one page it points to, near the end>
- FAQs: <the questions, if the profile wants them>
- Claims to verify: <every platform fact, price, policy, statistic>
- Open questions for the user: <only those that would change the piece>
```

## Notes

- One keyword per piece. A second phrase with the same intent is a variant,
  used in an H2; a phrase with a different intent is a different article.
- "What we add" is the field that decides whether the piece is worth
  writing. Matching the ranking pages earns parity at best; the addition is
  the reason to reorder them.
